# Adjustment Factor (Leadership Factor)

The adjustment factor describes **how demanding the project organization is beyond size and basic complexity**.  
It captures organizational, procedural, and coordination effort that cannot be derived solely from determining variables.

It **does not reward ambition, quality, or design excellence**.  
It models **leadership and management effort**.

---

## What is the adjustment factor?

The adjustment factor is a **multiplicative factor** applied to the base effort calculated from determining variables, use, and requirements.

It represents the additional effort arising from:
- coordination of multiple stakeholders,
- management of decision-making structures,
- handling of procedural uncertainty,
- leadership of complex planning organizations.

In short: it answers the question  
*“How demanding is this project to manage?”*  
not  
*“How good is the project?”*.

---

## Why does the ValueApp include the adjustment factor?

Projects with identical size and comparable requirements can generate very different effort due to:
- fragmented responsibilities,
- unclear or changing decision-making processes,
- tight schedules with many interfaces,
- unstable or evolving requirements from clients or users.

Without an adjustment factor, these differences would not be visible in the model and would be systematically underestimated.

The adjustment factor is used to:
- make leadership and coordination effort explicit,
- avoid unrealistic base assumptions,
- enable transparent discussion of organizational risks.

---

## What influences the adjustment factor?

The factor is derived from a structured assessment of **leadership and process conditions**. Typically, the following aspects are considered:

- **Organizational structure**  
  Number of stakeholders, hierarchies, contractual models.

- **Clarity of decision-making**  
  Clear responsibilities vs. distributed or changing accountability.

- **Process stability**  
  Fixed scope and schedule vs. iterative or open processes.

- **Coordination intensity**  
  Number and type of interfaces between disciplines, authorities, users, and external actors.

These assessments are qualitative and mapped onto a calibrated adjustment range.

## How does the adjustment factor affect the results?

The adjustment factor:
- scales the entire calculated effort,
- affects **all phases**, not only early design stages,
- increases effort ranges in cases of high uncertainty.

A higher adjustment factor generally means:
- more coordination effort,
- more alignment loops and meetings,
- higher leadership and management effort.

It:
- does **not** change the scope of services,
- does **not** guarantee higher fees,
- does **not** replace professional judgment.

## Where does the adjustment factor appear in the app?

You will encounter the adjustment factor in the ValueApp in several places:

- **Project definition**  
  As part of organizational and process-related inputs.

- **Service overview**  
  Where the total effort is adjusted accordingly.

- **Results overview**  
  Where its impact is visible in total hours and ranges.

## How to use the adjustment factor effectively

Use the adjustment factor to:
- address leadership effort early,
- explain differences between comparable projects,
- document assumptions in a transparent way.

Do **not** use it to:
- artificially increase results,
- compensate for missing service definitions,
- create a false sense of precision.

If you feel the urge to “tune” the factor until the result fits, this is usually a sign that the project organization has not yet been sufficiently clarified.
