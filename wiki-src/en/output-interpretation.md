# Interpreting Results

The results generated by the ValueApp are **decision support**, not promises, guarantees, or contractual truths.

They are intended to:
- structure early discussions,
- create shared expectations,
- make assumptions transparent.

They do **not** replace professional judgment.

---

## What the result represents

The ValueApp provides:
- an **effort range** (not a single value),
- broken down by **planning phases** and **scope of services**.

This range reflects:
- uncertainty in early project phases,
- differences in leadership and coordination effort,
- varying levels of process stability.

![Effort range instead of single value](\/wiki-assets/knowledge/output-effort-range.jpeg)

If you expect precision, you are usually too early in the project.

---

## Why the result is a range

Planning effort cannot be calculated deterministically because:
- requirements change,
- decisions are revised,
- coordination intensity fluctuates.

The range represents:
- **lower bound**: stable, favorable conditions,
- **upper bound**: increased coordination and iteration effort.

The width of the range is as important as its midpoint.

A narrow range usually indicates:
- a clearly defined project setup.

A wide range often indicates:
- unresolved organizational or process-related questions.

---

## Phase-based distribution of effort

The results show how effort is distributed across planning phases.

This helps to:
- understand the **timing** of service delivery,
- align resource and cash flow planning,
- compare projects structurally rather than emotionally.

![Effort distributed by phase](\/wiki-assets/knowledge/output-phase-distribution.jpeg)

Important:
- phase distributions are **model-based**, not schedules.
- they assume continuous progress, not real interruptions.

---

## Comparing projects

Two projects can be:
- similar in size,
- similarly ambitious,
- but produce different results.

Typical reasons:
- different roles or scopes of services,
- different organizational setups,
- different adjustment or leadership factors.

![Similar projects, different results](\/wiki-assets/knowledge/output-comparison-scenarios.jpeg)

Comparisons are only meaningful if:
- assumptions are comparable,
- roles and services match,
- the project phase is similar.

---

## What the result does *not* say

The results:
- do not define fees,
- do not guarantee effort,
- do not replace contracts,
- do not assign responsibility.

They:
- do not justify underfunding,
- do not compensate for unclear scope,
- do not eliminate project risk.

If a value seems “wrong”, check the assumptions first.

---

## Responsible use of results

Good practice:
- communicate ranges, not just averages,
- document key assumptions,
- update calculations as the project evolves.

Avoid:
- fixing numbers too early,
- presenting results as binding commitments,
- masking uncertainty with apparent precision.

Precision reassures. Transparency helps.
